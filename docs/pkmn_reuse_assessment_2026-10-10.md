**The recommendation is to reuse selected tools, while keeping the bot's current Champions rules engine and decision system.** The strongest candidate is a second battle-state reader from `pkmn/ps`; the simplest useful improvement is a repeatable, exact-format statistics importer. Neither requires replacing the whole bot.

This assessment compares the inspected checkout at `974450e` with public upstream documentation, source, published package metadata, and small isolated runtime checks on October 10, 2026. Our target is Pokémon Video Game Championships (VGC), Champions Regulation M-C. A package means a separately installable piece of reusable code. "Usable" means that a published, implemented tool can perform its documented job; it does not mean we have established that it can replace our Champions-specific implementation. The existing bot tests and historical audit results cited below were not rerun for this research.

The initial overview missed a useful fact: `@pkmn/mods` includes Champions mechanics. The narrower problem is that its published version is older than our current Regulation M-C rules and data. There is a credible adaptation path, but no evidence supporting an immediate simulator replacement.

**Our system already covers the essential jobs.** Its target is Pokémon Video Game Championships (VGC), Champions Regulation M-C: doubles, six team members with four selected, level 50, Mega Evolution, and no opponent team sheet on the best-of-one ladder. [Current format and data paths](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/src/vgc/config.py:20).

Champions uses Stat Points rather than ordinary effort values, the training allocations used in most Pokémon games. The allowed allocation is at most 32 per stat and 66 total. At level 50, health is `base + points + 75`; other stats use nature-adjusted `base + points + 20`. Merely accepting a field named `evs` is not enough: packed Champions teams use that field name for a different calculation. [Formula and validation](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/src/vgc/stats.py:103), [Champions constraints](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/CLAUDE.md:53).

**The best investigation is `@pkmn/client` plus `@pkmn/protocol`.** `protocol` reads Showdown's structured messages; `client` updates its understanding of Pokémon, sides and field conditions using those messages, including the player's own request. This is comparable to having a second scorekeeper read the same match. It is useful because a bot can choose the wrong move when its picture of the battle is wrong. [Client documentation](https://github.com/pkmn/ps/tree/main/client), [protocol documentation](https://github.com/pkmn/ps/tree/main/protocol).

It could eventually replace the part of poke-env that reads battle state, with an adapter that translates the output into our existing Python objects. It does not supply our strategy, legal doubles order enumeration, Showdown connection lifecycle, or exact simulator reconstruction. We already use Node for the simulator; this would add another package and data-conversion boundary rather than introduce Node for the first time.

The migration is substantial. Twenty-four source files directly import poke-env. That shows how connected the code is, rather than estimating effort: not all require rewriting, and indirect dependencies also exist. `VgcPlayer` processes messages one at a time so a decision cannot see later information in the same network burst. Its decision worker keeps the network connection responsive. Legal choices come from per-slot poke-env orders, and send-time corrections convert certain move and switch names into request positions. [Message timing](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/src/vgc/agent.py:166), [decision worker](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/src/vgc/agent.py:133), [legal doubles orders](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/src/vgc/actions.py:34), [switch encoding](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/src/vgc/actions.py:93).

The current reader is repaired rather than untouched poke-env. The saved October 9 audit reports 11,186 state-mismatch episodes without repairs, versus 153 with repairs across 2,400 battles. The remaining 153 were 88 documented limitations and 65 legitimately hidden opponent-item cases; zero were unexplained. These are historical audit measurements, not a new result and not proof of perfect parsing. Known limits include extra third types, some ability-swap/Illusion cases and other explicitly listed cases. [Audit record](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/.agents/worklog.md:340), [known limits](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/offline/audit_battle_parsing.py:962).

For that reason, begin with an offline comparison reader. Feed exactly the player's ordered messages and own requests into both readers. Compare at each decision against the existing Showdown-based audit. Supply current local Champions data rather than trust the published vanilla dex. Keep opponent facts separate from estimates, and never pass the simulator's hidden opponent team to the candidate reader. This could expose a maintenance advantage; it does not establish one in advance.

Adoption would require checks of changed Mega forms/abilities; Psych Up's copied boosts; temporary effects; sleep/toxic/Protect counters; weather, terrain and screen durations; team preview and team-sheet rejection; partial forced switches; locked moves; transformed switch targets; and decision-time cutoffs. Require zero unexplained differences, retain justified hidden-state exceptions, and measure per-decision time and process failure behavior. A faster or simpler reader must still preserve legal actions. Replacing transport or order generation would require separate verification; it is not part of a reader-only trial.

**The most straightforward improvement is repeatable statistics fetching.** Our current raw M-C file records `gen9championsvgc2026regmc`, cutoff 1760 and 1,631,943 battles. The source month is September 2026, documented by the existing builder and present in the official archive. That is already a substantial exact-format source. The number means broad coverage of that month's ladder, not 1.63 million independent high-rated teams. The rating cutoff is the statistics weighting setting, not proof that every included battle was above that rating. [Current raw source](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/data/usage/gen9championsvgc2026regmc-1760.json), [builder source description](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/tools/build_usage_spreads.py:35), [official September archive](https://www.smogon.com/stats/2026-09/).

The `smogon` repository offers two approaches. Its low-level `smogon` package builds direct source requests; its richer `@pkmn/smogon` package fetches processed data from `data.pkmn.cc`. The latter documents that changing VGC regulations may be grouped and served previous-series data. Source code also reduces some format identifiers to a base name. That behavior conflicts with our need for exact regulation provenance. [Direct-source wrapper](https://github.com/pkmn/smogon/tree/main/smogon), [processed-data client](https://github.com/pkmn/smogon/tree/main/pkmn).

My recommendation is a small Python importer for the exact official source URL and existing JSON shape. We can reuse the low-level package's endpoint knowledge without taking on a Node dependency for a single download. If there is a concrete reason to use the library, pass the exact month, exact format and explicit 1760 setting. Do not depend on automatic "best date", generic VGC format selection, or a replacement regulation.

The importer should record format, source month, weighting cutoff, battle count, retrieval time, URL and a content hash, a digital fingerprint of the downloaded file; validate every allocation under Champions limits; and refuse mismatches. The raw source contains useful metadata, but generated `spreads.json` currently does not retain those source details. This is a real, bounded improvement in repeatability. It does not replace `build_usage_spreads`, `vgc.sets`, replay-derived move/item/ability estimates, or legality checks. [Existing conversion](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/tools/build_usage_spreads.py:80), [current opponent-estimate loader](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/src/vgc/sets.py:63).

**`@pkmn/stats` is useful when we need reports the current aggregator does not produce.** The core package is independently published; the private shared-repository root does not make it unpublished. Its strengths are usage, leads, movesets, teammates and aggregate play-style reports. The sibling anonymizer `@pkmn/anon` is also published, contrary to the older FAQ's publication status. The massive log-processing framework `@pkmn/logs` is not published under that package name and remains unfinished in the inspected documentation. Do not treat all three as one finished product. [Core package](https://github.com/pkmn/stats/tree/main/stats), [anonymizer](https://github.com/pkmn/stats/tree/main/anon), [current registry evidence](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/runs/research/pkmn_20261010/registry-evidence.json).

Its parser expects stored simulator logs with metadata beyond the public replay text, including team information. Our public replays usually cannot provide complete hidden sets. Adapting a public replay into a server log by inventing those fields would corrupt the resulting report. Use compatible logs from our own explicitly offline experiments, with supplied teams, or make a tested public-replay adapter that marks missing information and limits which metrics can be reported. Outputs from private simulator logs must not become live opponent knowledge or exact public training targets.

Keep `replay_parse` for decision records. A replay shows events that executed; it does not always reveal the original submitted move or target. Missing actions must remain unknown. The local parser also preserves format identity and separates win, loss, draw and unresolved outcomes. A general statistics parser does not promise those semantics. [Replay contract](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/docs/replay_label_contract.md:10), [format enforcement](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/tools/parse_replays.py:55).

**Other implemented tools are usable but do not currently justify a migration.** `@pkmn/sets` is a mature team/set parser, but poke-env already reads teams and our worker uses the real Showdown `Teams.pack`. A new formatter must preserve Champions points and current Mega names and still use the actual validator. `@pkmn/login` would duplicate existing authentication; it does not replace the connection lifecycle. `@pkmn/streams` supplies message-stream utilities we already obtain from Showdown. `@pkmn/types` and `@pkmn/dex-types` are TypeScript development definitions, generally re-exported by higher-level packages; they offer little to a Python strategy. [Sets](https://github.com/pkmn/ps/tree/main/sets), [login](https://github.com/pkmn/ps/tree/main/login), [package guidance](https://github.com/pkmn/ps/blob/main/FAQ.md).

The same applies to `@pkmn/view` and `@pkmn/img` for a future battle viewer: useful display code, with no direct effect on move quality. `eslint-config` applies to JavaScript/TypeScript style, while the current main code uses Python and Ruff. `.github` is project administration. `randbats` and `@pkmn/randoms` are genuinely useful in Random Battles, but generated-team probabilities do not measure real M-C player choices. [Random Battle data](https://github.com/pkmn/randbats), [display components](https://github.com/pkmn/ps).

The published `@pkmn/spreads` component inside EPOke is real, but it uses conventional nature/effort-value/individual-value stat allocations. Keep our Champions formula and validation. Its range-display concepts can inspire a future explanation screen. The unfinished prediction packages are not a ready hidden-set estimator. [Spread component](https://github.com/pkmn/EPOke/tree/main/spreads).

**Do not replace the current simulator with the packaged one yet.** `@pkmn/sim` is an extraction of Showdown, not a different strategic engine, so its existence does not imply faster search. The current exact worker relies on cloning from serialized state, state patching, battle queues, move counters and controlled repeated-Protect outcomes. A packaged simulator has to preserve those contracts as well as legal format data. [Worker implementation](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/tools/sim_worker.mjs:330), [public state reconstruction](/Users/edmundyu/code/projects/pokemon-vgc-ai/tools/sim_worker.mjs:813), [simulator package](https://github.com/pkmn/ps/tree/main/sim).

Porting current Champions rules into the package is technically possible. It would also create a second distribution to synchronize with the real ladder source. We already pin, build, export and check the current Showdown checkout; adding another engine copy has no demonstrated benefit. A browser deployment or a separately packaged TypeScript product could change that tradeoff. Neither is required to improve the current Python bot.

The separate `pkmn/engine` is a low-level early-generation engine, not a modern M-C replacement. `dmg` leaves its damage calculation unfinished. `0-ERROR` does not supply a completed competitive bot, and PocketMon's proposed interface remains a prototype. `ai` supplies research and references. None establishes a ready replacement for our search, evaluator, damage model or training/data contracts. [Engine status](https://github.com/pkmn/engine#status), [unfinished calculator](https://github.com/pkmn/dmg/blob/main/src/mechanics/index.ts#L104-L125), [0-ERROR](https://github.com/pkmn/0-ERROR), [PocketMon code](https://github.com/pkmn/PocketMon/blob/main/src/index.tsx), [research site](https://github.com/pkmn/ai).

The recommended sequence is: first make exact-source statistics refreshes reproducible if that manual step is troublesome; next compare a second battle-state reader offline if parser maintenance is a priority; add the core statistics package only for a named report we cannot currently produce. Move from comparison to replacement only after the candidate handles our actual format and demonstrates a concrete correctness or maintenance advantage. Any claim of better playing strength still requires a controlled battle comparison and public ladder evidence.
