# Transformer v2 (`replica_v2_m192_l6_748t`)

> **Takeaway:** `v2` scaled the brain up 2.5× (`192×6`, 2.87M params) and expanded the team pool to 748 teams over 500,000 games, but **underperformed `v1`** (27.5% vs `v1`'s 36.5% against Python `vgc`, and lower win-chance accuracy). We never put `v2` on the public ladder — instead, its post-mortem uncovered four core flaws that led directly to our native Rust `VecEnv` engine and `v3`.

## 2. What We Changed from v1

- **Bigger Transformer:** Widened from `128×4` (1.16M params) to `192×6` (2.87M params).
- **Automatic cosine learning-rate schedule:** Added smooth decay from `1.5e-4 -> 1.5e-5` across the 500k games instead of manual restarts.
- **Expanded team pool:** Grew from 226 to 748 teams (`v2_expanded_train_manifest.json`).
- **Larger update window:** Fixed `games_per_update=256`, `snapshot_games=4096`, `eval_every=4096` (40 games each vs `heuristic` and `vgc`).

---

## 3. Training Hyperparameters & Throughput

- **PPO settings:** `batch_size=64`, `epochs=3`, `learning_rate=1.5e-4 -> 1.5e-5` (cosine), `league_size=24`, no KL early stopping, no anchor opponents, no GAE (Generalized Advantage Estimation — a way to credit earlier turns for setting up a win).
- **Throughput:** Started at `11.0 games/sec` (`208.8 decisions/sec`), peaked at `24.85 games/sec`, averaged **`22.3 games/sec`** late (~8.8 hours for 500k games — roughly 40% slower than `v1` because the model was 2.5× larger on CPU).

## 5. Post-Mortem: Why v2 Underperformed

Our post-mortem isolated **four root causes** — every one of which was fixed before `v3`:

1. **Unstable early updates & small-batch overfitting:** `approx_kl` spiked to **0.2057** (huge strategy whipsaws; healthy PPO stays under ~0.025) because `batch_size=64` with `epochs=3` and no KL early-stop overfit each batch of 256 games.
2. **Diluted team pool (369/748 stale or synthetic teams):** Nearly half the 748 teams had outdated Regulation M-B stat spreads or synthetic archetype templates, teaching the bot bad habits that didn't transfer to real Regulation M-C holdout teams.
3. **Board blind spots:** The `v1` board encoder lacked explicit turn timers (weather, Trick Room, terrains, Tailwind, screens), `protect_counter`, `first_turn` (Fake Out), and status counters — forcing the bot to guess timers it couldn't see.
4. **CPU throughput wall (~22 games/sec):** At 22 games/sec, 500k games took nearly 9 hours — far too slow to train a 2.87M-parameter brain across hundreds of teams (which needed millions of games, not 500k).

---

## 6. Direct Impact: The A+B+C+D + Native Rust Rewrite

`v2` was our most informative failed experiment. Its post-mortem triggered:
- **Clean 379-team M-C pool** (0 synthetic/M-B teams, 0 holdout overlap).
- **Rich Rust encoder + native `pikapro.rl.VecEnv`** running on Apple M4 Pro MPS GPU (~100 games/sec — **4.5× faster than `v2`**).
- **Proper PPO math:** GAE (`gamma=0.99, lambda=0.95`), BCE (binary cross-entropy — standard probability loss) value head, `batch_size=512`, `epochs=2`, KL early stopping (`target_kl=0.025`), and `simple_heuristics` anchor opponents.
